## Chapter 2 — 5 sub-chapters

| 1 | Morning Light on Stone | Dr. Tayak Moyaone | Tayak arrives at the excavation site before dawn, still unsettled by yesterday's vision of Red Fire Woman. Working by lamplight, she uncovers a water-smoothed river stone nestled against the copper pendant's resting place — its surface polished to near-mirror finish, warm despite the cold earth. She photographs it in situ, documents its position, and notes with scientific precision that its placement beside the pendant appears intentional rather than accidental. |
| 2 | The Weight of Water | Dr. Tayak Moyaone | Tayak lifts the stone and examines it: unusual warmth, perfect fit in her palm, wear patterns consistent with centuries of river tumbling. She records its composition and spatial relationship to the pendant in her field notes, but finds herself writing phrases that don't belong in an archaeological log — 'as if shaped for human hands,' 'the Potomac seems louder this morning.' She crosses them out. Writes them again. The river's sound grows insistent behind her. |
| 3 | The River Speaks | Dr. Tayak Moyaone (experiencing Speaks with Rivers) | When Tayak holds both the stone and the pendant together, the excavation site dissolves. She is pulled into a vision of the Potomac in 50 BC — pristine, teeming, bordered by unbroken forest. A young woman, Speaks with Rivers, stands waist-deep in the current, face painted for ceremony, arms raised in the third day of a vision quest. Tayak experiences the ancestor's hunger, exhaustion, and sharpened spiritual sensitivity as if they are her own. |
| 4 | The Memory Keeper's Burden | Speaks with Rivers (witnessed by Tayak) | Still in vision: Speaks with Rivers dives to the riverbed and retrieves Red Fire Woman's copper pendant, lost during a crossing fifty years prior. The moment she closes her fingers around it, she receives a flood of ancestral memory — faces, ceremonies, the chain of mothers stretching backward and forward through time. She returns to her village, presents the pendant to the elders, and is formally named memory keeper. She pairs the pendant with the river stone that guided her to it, speaks a promise aloud to daughters not yet born, and buries the bundle in the sacred mound. |
| 5 | River's Blessing | Dr. Tayak Moyaone | The vision releases Tayak back into the present. She walks to the Potomac's edge — the same bank where Speaks with Rivers once stood — and kneels to touch the water. The river is cold and immediate and real. She understands she is not the pendant's owner but its next guardian, chosen across two millennia. She whispers a promise of her own. Walking back to her vehicle, she notices her field notebook is open to a page she doesn't remember writing: a name, in her own handwriting — *Speaks with Rivers* — and below it, a question she cannot yet answer: *Who buried the stone the second time?* |
