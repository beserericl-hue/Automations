# Outline — The Burial Mound

*Engine brainstorm (title-locked), 67 chapters · 7 characters · 333.0s · status=complete.*

**Title (locked to DB):** The Burial Mound

**Premise** — Dr. Tayak Moyaone, a Piscataway archaeologist fighting for tribal recognition, excavates a burial mound in Charles County, Maryland. As she uncovers artifacts spanning over two millennia—from copper pendants to colonial documents—each discovery triggers visions of her ancestors' lives, revealing the unbroken chain of heritage that connects her to the very people whose stories she seeks to preserve. Only when she reaches the deepest layer does she realize she has been excavating her own family's sacred ground.

**Story arc** — The Descent: Seven Strata, Seven Centuries, One Family

**Dramatic question** — Can Dr. Tayak Moyaone prove her people's unbroken presence on this land before the state seizes the burial mound and erases the last evidence of Piscataway continuity — even as the excavation forces her to confront that she is digging up her own ancestors?

**Wow factor** — Every artifact Tayak uncovers triggers a fully realized vision of a specific ancestor's life — so the reader simultaneously inhabits a 21st-century legal and scientific battle AND a layered series of ancient lives descending through time, each vision more intimate and more devastating than the last, until the deepest layer reveals a face Tayak recognizes from her grandmother's photographs.

**Themes**
- The violence of erasure and the persistence of memory
- Who owns the dead — science or kinship
- The cost of proof in a world that demands it before granting dignity
- Continuity of identity across colonial rupture
- The archaeologist as both instrument of recovery and instrument of desecration

## Characters (7)

### Dr. Tayak Moyaone
- **Role:** Protagonist, POV character
- **Archetype:** The scientist-warrior: a credentialed archaeologist who uses academic tools as weapons in a political fight for her people's survival.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She is meticulous and fearless in the field but is privately terrified of her own visions — she has never told anyone she experiences them, because she knows it would destroy her scientific credibility and she suspects it would confirm what her colleagues already whisper: that she is too close to the material to be objective.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Tayak sacrifice her scientific reputation and personal boundaries to testify that the mound is her family's sacred ground, knowing that doing so may win the tribe's recognition but end her career?
- **Description:** Piscataway Nation member, late 30s, assistant professor at University of Maryland. Grew up in La Plata, Charles County, raised by her grandmother Nora after her parents' deaths. Collects vintage topographic maps as a hobby. Has a close non-plot friendship with her colleague Dr. Priya Suresh, a South Asian-American geologist, with whom she argues about cricket and eats terrible campus food. Private worry: she has begun to suspect her grandmother knew exactly what was in that mound and never told her.

### Nora Moyaone
- **Role:** Secondary POV character; Tayak's grandmother and the keeper of oral tradition
- **Archetype:** The elder who holds the secret — not a wise mentor who dispenses answers, but a frightened old woman who has spent sixty years deciding whether to speak.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Nora is warm and funny in company but has a cold, absolute ruthlessness when she believes the tribe's survival is at stake — she once reported a tribal member to state authorities to prevent him from selling land, and she has never apologized for it.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Nora finally tell Tayak the full truth about the mound — including the secret she has kept since 1962 — before the excavation makes the truth irrelevant?
- **Description:** 82 years old, lives in the same La Plata house where she raised Tayak. Former schoolteacher. Makes elaborate quilts using patterns she says came from her own grandmother. Attends every tribal council meeting despite her arthritis. Private worry: she is beginning to lose words — not dramatically, just occasionally — and she is racing against her own mind to pass on what she knows.

### Garrett Slade
- **Role:** Antagonist; Maryland State Historic Preservation Officer
- **Archetype:** The bureaucrat as weapon — a man who genuinely believes in the rules he enforces and is therefore more dangerous than a corrupt villain.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Slade is not racist in any obvious way; he has published sympathetic papers on Indigenous land rights and genuinely admires Tayak's scholarship. His antagonism is structural: he believes the process must be followed, and following the process will destroy the mound.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Slade's faith in institutional process survive the moment he realizes the process itself is the instrument of erasure?
- **Description:** 55, white, third-generation Marylander whose own family farm sits two miles from the mound. Runs marathons. Has a daughter who is a tribal rights attorney in New Mexico, a fact he mentions too often. Reads history obsessively but only published history. Private worry: he suspects his own family's land title has a gap in it from the 1680s that he has never investigated.

### Marcus Redcloud
- **Role:** Tribal council chair; Tayak's political ally and occasional obstacle
- **Archetype:** The politician who has made too many compromises and knows it.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Marcus is an effective advocate in rooms Tayak can't enter, but he has a private agreement with a real estate developer that he has not disclosed to the tribe — not corrupt exactly, but a hedge, a backup plan, a thing he tells himself he will never use.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Marcus use his undisclosed leverage to save the mound, even though doing so will expose the compromise he has hidden from his own people?
- **Description:** 52, Piscataway, grew up in Prince George's County. Law degree from Howard. Coaches youth lacrosse on weekends. Drinks too much coffee and not enough water. Has known Tayak since she was a teenager and feels a complicated mix of pride and guilt about how hard her life has been.

### Dr. Elliot Voss
- **Role:** Tayak's department chair; scientific establishment voice
- **Archetype:** The gatekeeper who believes he is a guardian.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Voss has spent his career championing Indigenous archaeology in the abstract while consistently failing specific Indigenous archaeologists in the particular — and he has a gift for framing each failure as being for their own good.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Voss choose institutional protection or genuine advocacy when Tayak's findings put the university's federal funding relationship at risk?
- **Description:** 62, white, Harvard PhD. Has a famous monograph on Chesapeake pre-contact trade networks that Tayak's work is quietly dismantling. Plays classical guitar badly. Genuinely fond of Tayak in the way a person is fond of a protégé they have not fully understood.

### Kimi Moyaone
- **Role:** Tayak's younger cousin; tribal youth activist and digital media organizer
- **Archetype:** The next generation who refuses to wait.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Kimi is brilliant at mobilizing public attention but has a reckless streak — she will post something true but strategically catastrophic if she believes it needs to be said, and she does not always consult the people it will affect.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Kimi's public campaign save the mound or destroy Tayak's legal case by making the excavation a media circus before the evidence is secure?
- **Description:** 26, lives in DC, works remotely as a UX designer. Runs a tribal heritage social media account with 80,000 followers. Learned beadwork from Nora. Has a boyfriend Tayak has never met. Private worry: she suspects she is more useful to the cause as a symbol than as a person, and she is not sure how she feels about that.

### Wren Calvert
- **Role:** Tayak's graduate student field assistant; scientific conscience
- **Archetype:** The true believer in archaeological method who is slowly radicalized by what she witnesses.
- **Off-axis attribute:** Wren is white, from a wealthy Virginia family, and chose Indigenous archaeology specifically to atone for something she will not name — a family history she has researched but never confronted directly.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Wren's discovery about her own family's connection to the Piscataway dispossession force her to choose between her mentor's cause and her own family's legal interests?
- **Description:** 28, PhD candidate. Meticulous field notes. Runs a small Etsy shop selling hand-drawn artifact illustrations. Reads Victorian novels for comfort. Has a complicated relationship with her mother, who thinks archaeology is a waste of a good brain.
