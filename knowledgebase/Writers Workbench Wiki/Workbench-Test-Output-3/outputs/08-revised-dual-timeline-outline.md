# 08 — Revised outline (dual-timeline) via the new `revise-outline` op

*Produced by feeding the run-3 outline (67 ch) + the directive below into the engine's new `brainstorm revise-outline` op (PR #125). Result: **96 chapters**, **18 characters**, 593s, DEV. Title preserved; existing present-day cast preserved; 11 new past-timeline POV ancestors + interleaved past chapters added.*

## Revision directive (the brainstorm idea applied)

```
Transform this from a present-day novel with ancestral "visions" into a TRUE DUAL-TIMELINE
novel in which the present-day excavation story is interwoven, chapter by chapter, with the
FULLY DRAMATIZED LIVES of the ancestors who made and owned each artifact Tayak unearths.

For each major artifact in Tayak's excavation (the copper pendant, and the other artifacts
spanning two millennia down to ~100 BC), create a PARALLEL PAST STORYLINE told in full scenes
— not visions, not summaries, not flashback fragments — each with its own named POV character
who is as real, developed, and dimensional as Tayak. The deepest layer reaches ~100 BC: tell
the COMPLETE story of the WOMAN WHO OWNED THE COPPER PENDANT — her life, her people, her loves
and losses, how the pendant came to her, what it meant, and how it came to be buried in the mound.

Add approximately 63 NEW past-timeline chapters (so the novel roughly doubles in length),
interleaved with the present-day chapters so that each artifact Tayak finds in the present is
paired with the past chapters that tell how that artifact came about and the life of the person
behind it. Each past character gets a full arc: origin, the central relationships and conflicts
of their life, the events that produced or marked the artifact, and the moment the artifact
passed on or was buried.

Preserve the present-day storyline and ALL its existing characters (Tayak, Nora, the council,
the BIA reviewer, the engineer, etc.) and the title "The Burial Mound". Keep ONE coherent arc
that braids the two timelines: as Tayak digs DOWN through the strata in the present, the past
chapters move BACK through time (most recent ancestor first, the ~100 BC pendant-woman last and
deepest), so the two timelines converge on the founding act at the bottom of the mound — the
moment that explains everything. Tag each chapter's act/arc_point with which timeline and era it
belongs to. Renumber consecutively with Prologue 0 and a final Epilogue. Give EVERY past-timeline
POV character a full entry in `characters` with their era, role, and arc.
```

## Result

- **Title:** The Burial Mound
- **Arc:** The Descent: Seven Strata, Seven Centuries, One Family — and the Two-Thousand-Year Answer
- **Chapters:** 67 -> 96 (+29 past-timeline)
- **Characters:** 7 -> 18 (+11 ancestors)

## Characters

- **Dr. Tayak Moyaone** — Protagonist, present-day POV character. Piscataway Nation member, late 30s, assistant professor at University of Maryland. Grew up in La Plata, Charles County, raised by her grandmother Nora after her parents' deaths. Collects vintage topographic maps as a hobby. Has a close non-plot friendship with her colleague Dr. Priya Suresh, a South Asian-American geologist, with whom she argues about cricket and eats terrible campus food. Private worry: she has begun to suspect her grandmother knew exactly what was in that mound and never told her.
- **Nora Moyaone** — Secondary POV character; Tayak's grandmother and the keeper of oral tradition. 82 years old, lives in the same La Plata house where she raised Tayak. Former schoolteacher. Makes elaborate quilts using patterns she says came from her own grandmother. Attends every tribal council meeting despite her arthritis. Private worry: she is beginning to lose words — not dramatically, just occasionally — and she is racing against her own mind to pass on what she knows.
- **Garrett Slade** — Antagonist; Maryland State Historic Preservation Officer. 55, white, third-generation Marylander whose own family farm sits two miles from the mound. Runs marathons. Has a daughter who is a tribal rights attorney in New Mexico, a fact he mentions too often. Reads history obsessively but only published history. Private worry: he suspects his own family's land title has a gap in it from the 1680s that he has never investigated.
- **Marcus Redcloud** — Tribal council chair; Tayak's political ally and occasional obstacle. 52, Piscataway, grew up in Prince George's County. Law degree from Howard. Coaches youth lacrosse on weekends. Drinks too much coffee and not enough water. Has known Tayak since she was a teenager and feels a complicated mix of pride and guilt about how hard her life has been.
- **Dr. Elliot Voss** — Tayak's department chair; scientific establishment voice. 62, white, Harvard PhD. Has a famous monograph on Chesapeake pre-contact trade networks that Tayak's work is quietly dismantling. Plays classical guitar badly. Genuinely fond of Tayak in the way a person is fond of a protégé they have not fully understood.
- **Kimi Moyaone** — Tayak's younger cousin; tribal youth activist and digital media organizer. 26, lives in DC, works remotely as a UX designer. Runs a tribal heritage social media account with 80,000 followers. Learned beadwork from Nora. Has a boyfriend Tayak has never met. Private worry: she suspects she is more useful to the cause as a symbol than as a person, and she is not sure how she feels about that.
- **Wren Calvert** — Tayak's graduate student field assistant; scientific conscience. 28, PhD candidate. Meticulous field notes. Runs a small Etsy shop selling hand-drawn artifact illustrations. Reads Victorian novels for comfort. Has a complicated relationship with her mother, who thinks archaeology is a waste of a good brain.
- **Siosaia (called Saya)** — Past-timeline POV character; Stratum One ancestor, 1690s. Late 20s, Piscataway, daughter of a community leader. Speaks English and some Dutch from trading contacts. Has a husband, Okwi, who is a skilled canoe-builder and deeply skeptical of her legal strategy. Has a young daughter she is teaching to read. The glass trade bead Tayak finds in Stratum One belonged to Saya — it was a gift from a Lenape trading partner and the only thing she kept from her own mother.
- **Okwi** — Past-timeline secondary POV character; Saya's husband, 1690s. Early 30s. Builds the finest canoes on the Potomac watershed — his work is recognizable by a specific carved motif at the prow. Teaches young men the craft. Has a dry, dark humor that Saya finds infuriating and irresistible. The oilskin document Tayak finds in Stratum One is Saya's petition; Okwi is the one who wrapped it and buried it at the mound when the petition failed.
- **Matta** — Past-timeline POV character; Stratum Two ancestor, 1740s. 50s, Piscataway, grandson of Saya and Okwi. Has lived his entire life under colonial governance and has developed a sophisticated double life: he attends the colonial church on Sundays and leads traditional ceremonies on the other six days. Makes the finest ceramic pipes in the region — the pipe bowl Tayak finds is his. Has a granddaughter he is training as his successor.
- **Tessa (Tessahki)** — Past-timeline POV character; Matta's granddaughter, 1740s–1760s. Teenager to young adult across her arc. Learns the ceramic craft from Matta. Has a colonial-educated brother who has converted and with whom she has a painful, loving, irresolvable argument that lasts their entire lives. The shell gorget Tayak finds in Stratum Three was Tessa's — she made it herself and wore it at every ceremony she led.
- **Ahanu** — Past-timeline POV character; Stratum Four ancestor, circa 1200 CE — the shell gorget era. 40s, community leader, not a chief by title but by practice. Has three adult children, two of whom are considering leaving for a community to the north. Has a rival, a man named Sokan, who believes the drought is punishment for the community's failure to maintain the old ways — and who is not entirely wrong. The shell gorget Tayak finds was Ahanu's — she wore it at the ceremony that ended the drought's worst year.
- **Sokan** — Past-timeline secondary POV character; Ahanu's rival, circa 1200 CE. 50s, keeper of the oldest ceremonial traditions. Has been right about three community crises in a row and has never let anyone forget it. Has a daughter, Waya, who is Ahanu's closest friend, which makes the rivalry between Sokan and Ahanu a source of private agony for Waya. The antler figure Tayak finds in Stratum Five was carved by Sokan and given to Ahanu as a gesture of reconciliation after the drought broke.
- **Waya** — Past-timeline POV character; Sokan's daughter and Ahanu's friend, circa 1200–1220 CE. 30s. Has a husband and two young children. Teaches her children to hunt. Has a private grief: a third child who died in the drought's worst year, a loss she has not spoken of to anyone, including her husband. The copper pendant Tayak finds in Stratum Five — the Hopewell-influenced one — was Waya's, passed to her by Ahanu.
- **Kessik** — Past-timeline POV character; Stratum Six ancestor, circa 500 CE — the Archaic-to-Woodland transition. 30s, community leader's daughter, not yet a leader herself. Has traveled farther than anyone in her community — she has seen mounds built by communities to the north and west and has understood something about them that she cannot fully articulate yet. Has a younger brother, Pell, who thinks the mound project is a waste of labor and says so loudly. The carved antler figure Tayak finds in Stratum Six was placed there by Kessik at the mound's first ceremony.
- **Pell** — Past-timeline secondary POV character; Kessik's brother, circa 500 CE. Late 20s. Strongest person in the community by a significant margin. Has a gift for structural problem-solving — he is the one who figures out how to move the earth efficiently. Has a wife, Nima, who understood the mound's purpose before he did and has been quietly waiting for him to catch up.
- **Yanu** — Past-timeline POV character; the founding ancestor, circa 100 BC — the copper pendant woman. Late 30s. Has four living children and one dead one — a daughter, Taya, who died in her second year. The copper pendant was made for Taya by a Hopewell-connected trader who passed through the community; Yanu wore it after Taya's death and buried it with her daughter. Has a husband, Ranu, who grieves differently — loudly, communally — and does not understand Yanu's silence. Has a gift for reading weather from the behavior of birds, a skill she is teaching her oldest surviving daughter.
- **Ranu** — Past-timeline secondary POV character; Yanu's husband, circa 100 BC. 40s. Tells stories at every communal gathering. Has a reputation for embellishment that is both celebrated and distrusted. Has three surviving children with Yanu and a stepson from a previous relationship. Carves small figures from antler as a meditative practice — the figures he makes after Taya's death are different from all the others: simpler, more still.
