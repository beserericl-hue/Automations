The Charles County Government Building sat on Washington Avenue between a nail salon and a parking garage, its brick facade decorated with a flag that had gone stiff and pale in the November wind. Slade pulled into the garage at 11:40, took a ticket, and sat for a moment with the engine running.

The printout from Marcus's legal team lay on the passenger seat. He had read it four times that morning at his desk, each time stopping at the same line in the boundary description: *bounded to the north and east by the Piscataway settlement at the high ground adjacent to Mattawoman Creek.* The 1687 Calvert grant. Two hundred acres called Friendship.

He picked up the printout, folded it along its existing crease, and put it in his coat pocket.

The records office was on the ground floor, past a security desk where a young man in a blue uniform asked Slade to sign in and slide his state ID under the plexiglass. "Help you?"

"Title chain on a parcel. Charles County. I'm looking at a tract that goes back to at least 1683, possibly earlier."

"Parcel ID?"

He gave it. He had pulled it from the county assessor's portal that morning, sitting at his desk with the door closed, before Phil had started his rounds at the Trust.

She typed without looking at him. "You want the full chain?"

He got out of the car.

The cold came in immediately, thirty-nine degrees, the smell of November leaves and the neighbor's woodsmoke and the faint diesel exhaust from the road. He stood on the gravel apron for a moment. The reflective address numbers Carol had put up caught the streetlight. The kitchen window was bright and warm and Carol was moving past it again.

He had not pulled the chain forward from Dent. He did not know yet whether Harold Slade's name was at the end of it. He had a suspicion and two photocopied pages and a notebook entry, and he was going to go inside and he was going to call his daughter and tell her what he had found, and he was going to tell her that he had not finished the work, that there was more to pull, that he had stopped before he was ready to know the rest of it.

He did not know what she would say.

He had tried to build that conversation in the garage and on the highway and in the driveway and he had failed each time. He was going to have to stand at the edge of it and find out.

He walked to the door.
