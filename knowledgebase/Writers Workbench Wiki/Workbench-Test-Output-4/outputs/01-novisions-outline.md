**Arc:** The Descent: Seven Strata, Seven Centuries, One Family — and the Two-Thousand-Year Answer Carried in Spoken Words

**Premise:** Dr. Tayak Moyaone, a Piscataway archaeologist fighting for tribal recognition, excavates a burial mound in Charles County, Maryland. As she uncovers artifacts spanning over two millennia, each discovery is paired with the fully dramatized life of the ancestor who made or owned it — lives reached not through visions but through the chain of oral tradition: stories told at fires, on deathbeds, in letters, in song, each generation handing the next a story about the one before, carrying the reader backward through time from the 1690s to approximately 100 BC. In the present, Tayak fights the state, her institution, and her own family's silence. In the past, her ancestors fight erasure, displacement, drought, war, and grief. The two timelines converge at the mound's deepest layer, where the woman who first buried the copper pendant performs the act that will echo forward two thousand years — and where Tayak, reading Nora's final affidavit in the trench, understands for the first time that she has been excavating her own family's ground.

**Dramatic question:** Can Dr. Tayak Moyaone prove her people's unbroken presence on this land before the state seizes the burial mound — and will the chain of oral stories her grandmother has kept, each one dramatizing a real ancestor's life going back to 100 BC, constitute the proof that no court, no institution, and no act of erasure can finally destroy?
