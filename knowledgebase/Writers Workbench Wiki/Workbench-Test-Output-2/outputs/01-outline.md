# Outline — The Deepest Layer
*Engine brainstorm, op=story, genre=ancient-history, streamed @ max_tokens=32768. 59 chapters · 9 characters · 242s · status=complete.*

**Premise** — A Piscataway archaeologist excavating a burial mound in Charles County, Maryland uncovers artifacts spanning two millennia, each triggering ancestral visions that gradually reveal she is excavating her own family's sacred ground—and that the evidence she needs to win federal tribal recognition lies buried beneath the bones of her own ancestors.

**Story arc** — The Mound

**Dramatic question** — Can Tayak excavate the burial mound—layer by layer, vision by vision—and surface the unbroken chain of Piscataway heritage before the county condemns the land for a highway interchange, and before she must decide whether to sacrifice her ancestors' remains to win the federal recognition that will save her people?

**Wow factor** — Every artifact Tayak uncovers triggers a fully realized vision of a specific ancestor's life—rendered in present-tense immediacy—so the reader simultaneously experiences the archaeological dig in 2024 and the living Piscataway world across twenty-two centuries, making the burial mound itself the novel's second protagonist.

**Themes**
- The violence of erasure and the labor of remembrance
- Whether scientific method and sacred obligation can coexist
- The cost of recognition—what must be surrendered to be seen
- Continuity of identity across colonial rupture
- The ethics of disturbing the dead to save the living

## Characters (9)

### Dr. Tayak Moyaone
- **Role:** Protagonist, POV character
- **Archetype:** The scholar-warrior: a trained archaeologist who is also the person most personally threatened by what she might find.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She is meticulous and fearless in the field but catastrophically avoidant in personal confrontation—she will face a state bulldozer without flinching and then fail to tell her own mother she loves her.
- **Dramatic question:** Can Tayak use the mound's evidence to win federal recognition without destroying the sacred ground—and the family secret—that gives her people their identity?
- **Description:** Forty-one years old, Piscataway Conoy, raised in La Plata, Maryland. Holds a PhD in historical archaeology from the University of Maryland. Works as an adjunct at St. Mary's College while running the tribal historic preservation office on a shoestring budget. Hobby: competitive canoe paddling on the Potomac, which she does alone at dawn. Private worry: she suspects her late father falsified part of the genealogical record used in the tribe's previous failed recognition bid, and she has never looked closely enough to confirm it. Friend outside the plot: Darnell Swann, a Black waterman from Cobb Island who trades her blue crabs for whatever she is reading. Humiliated by: being told by a federal official that her tribe 'doesn't exist.' Terrified by: opening the deepest burial and finding evidence that destroys rather than confirms the lineage. Made happy by: the moment a student first understands that dirt is a document.

### Councilman Garrett Posey
- **Role:** Antagonist, secondary POV
- **Archetype:** The pragmatic local politician who is not evil but whose interests are structurally opposed to Tayak's at every turn.
- **Off-axis attribute:** He genuinely believes in historic preservation—he funded the county museum—but only for history that attracts tourism dollars; living Indigenous claims make him viscerally uncomfortable in a way he has never examined.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Posey sacrifice the highway project—and his political future—when he finally understands what the mound actually is?
- **Description:** Fifty-six, Charles County Commissioner, third-generation Southern Marylander, tobacco-farming family. Drives a pickup with a 'Buy Local' sticker and a Confederate-era regimental flag pin on his lapel that he considers purely historical. Has a genuine passion for Civil War battlefield preservation. His off-axis cruelty: he is unfailingly polite to Tayak's face and works to undermine her through procedure the moment she leaves the room. Dramatic question resolves at the climax when the mound's evidence forces a public choice.

### Nora Moyaone
- **Role:** Secondary POV, Tayak's mother
- **Archetype:** The keeper of secrets: the elder who knows what is buried and has spent forty years deciding whether to tell.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She presents as a gentle, church-going grandmother but has a steel capacity for strategic deception—she has been managing what the family knows and doesn't know about the mound since Tayak's father died.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Nora finally tell Tayak the truth about their family's connection to the mound before the excavation surfaces it violently?
- **Description:** Seventy-three, lives in the same house in La Plata where Tayak grew up. Worked thirty years as a medical records clerk; she understands the power of documentation and its absence. Keeps a cedar box under her bed that she has never shown Tayak. Hobby: quilting patterns that encode clan symbols she learned from her own grandmother. Private worry: that telling the truth will destroy Tayak's career and the recognition bid simultaneously.

### Marcus Redcloud
- **Role:** Supporting POV, field archaeologist
- **Archetype:** The idealistic graduate student who becomes a moral pressure on Tayak.
- **Off-axis attribute:** He is Lakota, not Piscataway, and brings a NAGPRA-hardened skepticism about whether any excavation of Indigenous remains is ever justified—which puts him in direct tension with Tayak, whose entire strategy depends on the dig.
- **Dramatic question:** Can Marcus reconcile his belief in repatriation with his loyalty to Tayak and the specific political necessity that drives her excavation?
- **Description:** Twenty-eight, PhD candidate at Maryland, assigned to Tayak's project. Excellent field technician. Plays chess online obsessively at night in the site trailer. Has a younger sister on the Pine Ridge reservation whose diabetes treatment depends on tribal health services—he understands viscerally what federal recognition means in material terms. His moral complication: he discovers the falsified genealogical record before Tayak does and must decide whether to tell her.

### Dr. Sylvia Crane
- **Role:** Supporting antagonist, federal BIA reviewer
- **Archetype:** The bureaucratic gatekeeper who is not corrupt but whose procedural rigidity functions as oppression.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She is herself part Cherokee, which makes her more demanding of Piscataway evidence, not less—she has seen too many fraudulent recognition bids and has overcorrected into prosecutorial skepticism.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Crane accept the mound's evidence as sufficient proof of continuous Piscataway presence, or will procedural standards erase the very people the law was designed to protect?
- **Description:** Fifty, Bureau of Indian Affairs, Branch of Acknowledgment and Research. Based in Washington. Meticulous, fair within her own framework, privately exhausted by a system she knows is broken. Has a habit of reading the petitions she rejects a second time, alone, after hours.

### Ancestor: Wren-Calling-Woman (Vision Layer 1, c. 200 CE)
- **Role:** Ancestral vision character
- **Archetype:** The founder: the woman who first consecrated the mound.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She is the tribe's most skilled healer and also the person who ordered the execution of a man whose burial she then oversaw with full ceremony—her compassion and her ruthlessness are inseparable.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Wren-Calling-Woman's act of consecration—and the copper pendant she buries—survive long enough to speak to her descendant two thousand years later?
- **Description:** Appears in Tayak's visions triggered by the copper pendant at the deepest layer. Her scenes establish the mound's origin, the clan's founding covenant, and the specific lineage marker that will become the key evidence in the recognition case.

### Ancestor: Osceola Moyaone (Vision Layer 2, c. 1650 CE)
- **Role:** Ancestral vision character
- **Archetype:** The diplomat in an impossible position: negotiating with English colonists while watching his world dissolve.
- **Off-axis attribute:** He speaks English better than he lets on and uses the colonists' assumption of his ignorance as a tactical weapon—but this same deception costs him the trust of his own people.
- **Dramatic question:** Can Osceola preserve the mound's location from colonial surveyors without surrendering the clan's autonomy?
- **Description:** Appears in visions triggered by a colonial-era brass trade token. His scenes dramatize the 1650s Piscataway diplomatic crisis, the Moyaone village relocation, and the deliberate act of concealment that hid the mound from English maps.

### Ancestor: Judith Moyaone (Vision Layer 3, c. 1790 CE)
- **Role:** Ancestral vision character
- **Archetype:** The survivor who passes the secret forward under the guise of Christianity.
- **Off-axis attribute:** She is a devout Catholic convert who uses the church's record-keeping system to hide Indigenous genealogical information inside baptismal records—a double agent of documentation.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Judith's encoded records survive long enough to be read correctly—and will anyone know how to read them?
- **Description:** Appears in visions triggered by a rosary bead found in the mound's upper stratum. Her scenes show the post-colonial erasure period, the deliberate encoding of clan identity in Catholic parish records, and the specific document that Tayak's father later found and partially falsified.

### Raymond Posey
- **Role:** Minor supporting character
- **Archetype:** Garrett Posey's adult son, a civil engineer on the highway project, who becomes an unexpected ally.
- **Off-axis attribute:** He studied anthropology for two years before switching to engineering and has a residual, guilty fascination with the mound that his father finds embarrassing.
- **Dramatic question:** Will Raymond use his position inside the highway project to buy Tayak time, and at what cost to his relationship with his father?
- **Description:** Thirty-one, works for the state highway administration as a project engineer. Attends the county commission meetings where the mound is discussed. His insider knowledge of the demolition timeline becomes critical in the novel's final act.
