## 1. Getting Started

The Writers Workbench is your **dashboard** for managing AI-written books, stories, blog posts, and newsletters. The system has two ways to give it instructions:

- **Eve (voice)** — click "Talk to Eve" in the sidebar and speak naturally.
- **Chat drawer (text)** — click the chat icon in the top bar and type.

The UI itself is for **viewing, editing, organizing, approving, and publishing** content. You will not find a "New Project" button anywhere — projects are created by asking Eve or the chat to brainstorm one.

> **About the Newsletter sidebar item.** You may notice a **Newsletter** entry in the sidebar that doesn't lead to any active screens yet. The full **Newsletter Agent** (ingestion, draft, review, scheduled send) is shipping in the next release. Until then, the entry is a placeholder, and the **RSS Feed URLs** field on the Genres screen (§12) is where you can pre-populate discovery sources so they're ready when the pipeline goes live. Existing newsletters generated by the prior V1 pipeline still appear in the Content Library and can be edited and emailed normally — only the newsletter *creation/scheduling UI* is pending.

### Production URL

```
https://writersworkbench-production.up.railway.app
```

### Browser Requirements

- Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox (latest two major versions)
- Microphone access (only required for Eve voice mode)
- Allow pop-ups from the production domain (for the .docx export download)

## 10. Brainstorm

URL: **`/brainstorm`**

![Brainstorm form, empty](./screenshots/10-brainstorm-form.png)

This is **how new projects are born.** Brainstorm takes a rough premise (or even just a tone + topic) and turns it into a full chapter-by-chapter outline saved as a new project. After you submit, the system:

1. Sends the request to the writing engine.
2. Returns an **acknowledgement** within a couple of seconds: *"Working on it — you'll get an email when it's ready."*
3. Runs the brainstorm in the background (typically 60–120 seconds).
4. Drops the finished outline into **My Projects → [your title]** and emails you a copy.

The form has three steps. We'll walk through each with a sample project — *Term Limits*, a fictional political-comedy novel — so you can see exactly what to type and why.

> **Tip:** If the genre or story arc you want isn't in the dropdown, create it first in **Reference → Genres** (§12) or **Reference → Story Arcs** (§13). The Brainstorm form only lists genres and arcs that already exist.

### Step 1 — Tell the system what the book is about

Two ways to start: paste text, or upload a document. **Pick one** — they're alternatives, not both.

#### Book Concept Text (textarea, up to 50,000 chars)

Paste anything you have about the book: premise, character notes, a few chapter ideas, scraps from a brainstorming session, even a synopsis of a similar book you want to riff on. The brainstorm engine reads all of it.

**What to put here:**

- **Premise** (1–3 sentences): the central conflict, in plain English.
- **Main characters** (3–5): name, role, one-line description.
- **Tone / influences**: what existing book or show is this in conversation with?
- **Suggested chapter structure** (optional): if you already know the beats.

**What NOT to put here:**

- Long Wikipedia-style backstory (the brainstorm doesn't need lore — it needs structure).
- Real people you know (the system will lift names verbatim into the outline).
- Existing copyrighted prose. Paraphrase it.

**Example for *Term Limits* (political comedy):**

```
Title: Term Limits

Premise: When a populist senator dies mid-vote, his estranged daughter — a stand-up
comedian who has spent a decade roasting him from a Brooklyn basement — is appointed
to fill his seat. Sworn in on a Tuesday, she discovers by Friday that her late father
had been blackmailing the Speaker of the House for fifteen years, and the leverage now
sits in a manila folder in her freshman office.

Main characters:
- Joey "JoJo" Halloran — 34, comedian, the new senator. Sharp, unimpressed, allergic
  to scripts.
- Speaker Margaret Vance — 61, calculating, has spent two decades being the smartest
  person in every room except her therapist's.
- Chief of Staff Wendell — 52, inherited from her father, knows where every body is
  buried and which of them are still breathing.
- Press Secretary Imani — 28, a former White House intern who treats the briefing
  room like a venue.

Tone: dry political satire in the tradition of Veep and Thank You for Smoking — not
mean, just unflinching. Comedy of competence and incompetence in equal measure.

Suggested chapter structure: 12 chapters covering swearing-in, discovery of the folder,
first leverage play, media leak, Speaker's counterattack, family revelation, midterm
election, climax in the Senate cloakroom.
```

#### Or Upload a Document

Drag a `.docx` or `.pdf` into the dashed area, or click to browse. The system runs the same parser that reads chapter uploads (under the hood it pulls plain text, drops formatting, then feeds it to the same Step-2 extractor).

Use this when your premise lives in a Word document already, or when a writing partner emailed you a treatment.

**Click "Analyze Content"** — the system pulls candidate values for the Step-2 fields out of your text. If anything looks wrong, you can override it in Step 2 before submitting.

### Step 2 — Review & Adjust

Once you've pasted text or uploaded a file (and clicked Analyze Content), the four review fields populate. You can edit any of them before submitting.

![Brainstorm form filled with the *Term Limits* example](./screenshots/10b-brainstorm-filled-political-comedy.png)

| Field | Required? | What to put here |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | ✅ | The book's working title. Will become the project name. Renameable later from the project workspace. |
| **Target Chapter Count** | optional | Integer, 1–100. Defaults to 12 if blank. The brainstorm engine uses this as a target, not a hard cap — it may produce a Prologue (chapter 0) and/or Epilogue (chapter N+1) on top. |
| **Genre** | ✅ | Pick from the dropdown. Genres are managed in **Reference → Genres** (§12). The brainstorm engine reads the genre's writing guidelines, sample sources, and style cues to shape the outline. If you don't see your genre, [create it first](#12-reference-genres). |
| **Story Arc** | ✅ | Pick from the dropdown. Story arcs are skeletal narrative frameworks — Three-Act Structure, Hero's Journey, Freytag's Pyramid, Kishōtenketsu, etc. Managed in **Reference → Story Arcs** (§13). Each arc imposes a structure on the chapter beats; you can override it later by re-brainstorming. |
| **Themes** | optional | Type a theme, press **Enter** (or click **Add**). Each theme becomes a chip you can dismiss with the × button. Themes are signals to the writer about what the book is *really about* underneath the plot — power, idealism vs cynicism, grief, etc. The brainstorm uses them to weave subtext into chapter summaries. |

**Themes for *Term Limits*:** `satire` `power` `media` `idealism vs cynicism`

The chips at the bottom of the screenshot show all four added. Themes don't have a hard limit, but 3–6 keeps the outline focused.

### Step 3 — Submit Brainstorm

Click the **Brainstorm** button at the bottom. You'll get a green toast: *"Command sent — results will appear in your Content Library."* The form clears.

Behind the scenes:

1. The writing engine validates the inputs and resolves the genre + story arc.
2. It runs research on the genre/topic to gather discovery material.
3. It generates a chapter outline using the writing prime directive, the genre's writing guidelines, the chosen story arc structure, and your concept text.
4. It saves the new project, attaches the outline, and emails you the rendered HTML.
5. The Content Library and **My Projects** sidebar refresh automatically.

If you don't see the email within 3 minutes, check **Reference → Cost Tracking** (§16) — failed brainstorms still log a cost row with an error in the description column. Common causes: invalid genre, story arc not found, or a transient model rate limit.

> **Want to revise an existing outline instead of starting fresh?** Don't use Brainstorm for that — use **Chat with Eve** (§20) or **Talk to Eve** (§21) and say *"revise the outline for `[title]` to add a prologue"* / *"change JoJo's age to 38"*. Re-brainstorming creates a new project; revision edits the one you have and version-snapshots the previous outline.

## 12. Reference: Genres

URL: **`/genres`**

![Genres list](./screenshots/12-genres-list.png)

A genre is a **reusable creative template**. Every brainstorm and writing workflow reads the genre's writing guidelines, keyword bank, and source URLs to shape its output. Adding a new genre is the single highest-leverage thing you can do to make the system write the kind of book *you* want — far more impactful than tweaking individual brainstorm prompts.

The page has two sections:

### Public Genres Section

Read-only system genres that ship with the platform (e.g., *post-apocalyptic*, *political-scifi*, *historical-time-travel*, *metaphysical-romance*, *scifi-romance*). Anyone can use them in brainstorms and writes. You can't edit or delete these — if you want a variation, copy one of them into a custom genre and tweak from there.

### Custom Genres Section

Your private genres. Full CRUD.

- **+ New Genre** button (top right).
- Each card shows: name, slug, description, **feed count**, **project count** (live), edit and delete buttons.
- **Delete** is blocked if the genre is in use — a cascade dialog tells you which projects/content currently reference it. Reassign or delete those first.

### Walkthrough — creating a "Political Comedy" genre

We'll create a brand-new genre called **Political Comedy** to support a satirical-fiction line. Click **+ New Genre** in the top right of the genres page. The list view is replaced by the create form (`+ Back to Genres` link returns).

#### Empty form

![New Genre form, empty state](./screenshots/12a-genre-form-empty.png)

Every field except Slug auto-populates from defaults; Slug is generated from Name as you type.

#### Filled form — top half (identity + sources)

![New Genre form, top — name / slug / description / keywords / RSS feeds / source URLs](./screenshots/12b-genre-form-filled-top.png)

| Field | Required? | What to put here |
|---|---|---|
| **Genre Name** | ✅ | Display name as it'll appear in dropdowns. *Political Comedy.* Title-case is conventional. |
| **Slug** | optional (auto-generated) | URL-safe key derived from Name on first edit. *political-comedy.* Override only if you want a shorter alias (e.g., `polcom`) — must stay lowercase, hyphenated, no spaces. The slug is what brainstorm requests pass over the wire, so renaming a genre slug after projects exist will break their genre reference. |
| **Description** | ✅ | One paragraph. Tell the system what the genre **is** — tone, register, influences, what makes it different from adjacent genres. The brainstorm engine reads this to anchor outline tone. *Sharp, character-driven satire set in and around modern political institutions — campaign trails, congressional hallways, war rooms, late-night greenrooms. Tone is dry and observational; the humor comes from competence colliding with vanity, not from caricature. Influences: Veep, Thank You for Smoking, Primary Colors, The Thick of It.* |
| **Keywords (comma-separated)** | optional | A search-style list. Used by Perplexity research to seed discovery queries when the brainstorm needs reference material. Keep it tight (5–10 terms). For Political Comedy: *political satire, dry humor, congressional intrigue, campaign trail, dark comedy, media circus, partisan absurdity.* |
| **RSS Feed URLs** | optional, URL-validated | Discovery feeds. Each URL becomes a polled source for the **Newsletter Agent** ingestion pipeline (a later release — populating these now means the genre is ready when that ships). For Political Comedy, three example feeds: `https://www.theonion.com/rss`, `https://www.mcsweeneys.net/rss`, `https://reductress.com/feed/`. Click **+ Add rss feed url** to add another row; use the up/down arrows to reorder; the red × removes a row. Invalid URLs get a red border with an error tooltip. |

> **About RSS Feed URLs.** This is the field most people ask about. Today, the Workbench reads these only for context when researching a genre. In the upcoming **Newsletter Agent** release, the same URLs will become *inbound* feeds — the system will poll them on a schedule, dedupe items, and surface fresh discovery material into your library so you can cite or repurpose it. Populate these even if you don't plan to use the newsletter pipeline today; future-you will thank present-you.

#### Filled form — bottom half (sources + writing rules + activate)

![New Genre form, bottom — Source URLs / Subreddit Names / Goodreads Shelves / Writing Guidelines / Active](./screenshots/12b-genre-form-filled-bottom.png)

| Field | Required? | What to put here |
|---|---|---|
| **Source URLs** | optional | Article/site URLs the brainstorm and research workflows can quote or link to. For Political Comedy: `https://www.politico.com/`, `https://www.thebulwark.com/`. Same Add / reorder / delete UI as RSS Feed URLs. |
| **Subreddit Names** | optional | Bare subreddit names — no `r/` prefix, no full URL. *PoliticalHumor*, *TheThickOfIt*. Reddit ingestion (when wired) reads these as community signals. |
| **Goodreads Shelves** | optional | Goodreads shelf identifiers used to surface real-world examples and bestseller lists for this genre. *political-satire*, *comedy*. |
| **Writing Guidelines** | ✅ | The single most important field. This is appended to every prompt sent to Claude when writing in this genre. Use it to tell the AI **what to do and what to avoid**. Cover: tone, register, what makes the humor land, banned habits (e.g., "no caricature", "no partisan editorializing"), pacing rules, audience assumption. |
| **Active** (checkbox) | — | Defaults to checked. Uncheck to soft-disable a genre — it stays in the database but disappears from brainstorm/write dropdowns. Use this instead of deleting when projects depend on the genre but you want to retire it from new work. |

**Sample Writing Guidelines for *Political Comedy*:**

> Tone: dry, observational, professional. Avoid caricature — characters should be competent at their jobs and the humor should emerge from their incentives, not from making them stupid. Avoid partisan editorializing — both parties should look human, foolish, and occasionally dignified by turns. Pacing: scene-driven, dialogue-heavy. Episodes should function like late-night sketches connected by character continuity. Assume an adult audience familiar with how legislative process actually works.

#### Submitting

Click **Create Genre** at the bottom-left of the form. The genre saves and you're returned to the genres list with the new card visible in the **Custom Genres** section. The genre is immediately selectable in the Brainstorm form (§10), Write Chapter, Repurpose, and every other genre-aware workflow.

> **Editing later.** Click the pencil icon on the card to re-open the same form pre-filled. Changes apply to **future** brainstorms and writes — content already produced under the genre is unaffected.

## 20. Chat with Eve (the text-based chat drawer)

![Chat with Eve drawer, open](./screenshots/20-chat-drawer-open.png)

Opens from the **chat icon** in the top bar (right side, between the search and theme toggle).

This is the **text version of Eve.** She shares the same brain, tools, and memory as the voice agent in §21 — both surfaces talk to the same writing engine and read/write the same projects, outlines, library, and story bibles. **Anything you can say to voice Eve, you can type to chat Eve.** The full command reference is in §22.

### When to use chat vs voice

| Use chat when… | Use voice when… |
|---|---|
| You need an exact title, quote, or URL pasted in (voice transcription is fuzzy) | You're hands-free or pacing |
| You're in a quiet space (open office, library, late at night) | You want to think out loud or revise mid-sentence |
| You want a written transcript to copy from later | You want Eve to read short results aloud |
| You want one-click **Quick Commands** to scaffold a request | You want a phone callback to discuss a chapter live |

You can also mix them — start a brainstorm in chat (paste your reference URL precisely), then ask voice Eve to "pull up that outline" once it lands. Both surfaces see the same history.

### Anatomy of the drawer

A resizable side panel anchored to the right edge of the window. Drag the **left edge** to resize anywhere from 360 px (cozy) to 800 px (transcript-comfortable).

#### Message History (top)

- **Persistent across navigation** — stored in `localStorage` per browser. Last 100 messages.
- Each message shows a **timestamp** on hover.
- **You** messages are right-aligned with a brand-tinted background; **Eve** messages are left-aligned with a neutral background.
- **Animated typing indicator** (three bouncing dots) appears while a tool is running. Long tools (brainstorm, write chapter) show this for 30–120 s — that's normal.
- **Clear history** button (top of drawer) wipes localStorage. Use it before sharing your screen if you don't want previous prompts visible.
- Scrolling up loads earlier messages from the same session. Reloading the page keeps the session.

#### Input (bottom)

- Auto-growing textarea — paste multi-line concepts, character lists, even an entire chapter.
- **Enter** sends. **Shift + Enter** for newline.
- The send button (paper-plane icon) lights up only when the input has non-whitespace content.

#### Quick Commands Panel

A grid of one-click prompt scaffolds that pre-populate the input box (so you can edit before sending). Standard commands:

- **List my projects**
- **Brainstorm a book…**
- **Write a chapter…**
- **Research a topic…**
- **Generate cover art…**
- **Repurpose for social…**
- **List my outlines**
- **Approve content…**

When you're inside a project workspace (`/projects/<id>`), **context-aware commands** appear in the same panel, pre-filled with the current project name:

- **Write next chapter of *"[Project Name]"***
- **Generate cover art for *"[Project Name]"***
- **Repurpose *"[Project Name]"* for social**

The Quick Commands are scaffolds, not magic — clicking one drops the template into the input box and you finish the sentence. *"Brainstorm a book…"* expands to a starter prompt; you complete it with the topic, genre, and any constraints, then hit Enter.

### Sample interactions

**Synchronous — list operations return inline:**

> **You:** List my outlines  
> **Eve:** Here are your 12 outlines:  
> 1. *The Invisible Wall* — post-apocalyptic, 14 chapters  
> 2. *The Burial Mound* — historical-time-travel, 11 chapters  
> 3. *Term Limits* — political-comedy, 12 chapters  
> …

**Asynchronous — write/brainstorm/repurpose return an ack, results land later:**

> **You:** Brainstorm a book in political-comedy. Premise: a stand-up comedian gets appointed to fill a senate seat after her father dies, and discovers the late senator was blackmailing the Speaker of the House.  
> **Eve:** Command sent — results will appear in your Content Library.  
> *(60–120 seconds later)*  
> **🔔 Toast notification** (bottom-right): *"Outline ready for Term Limits"*

The Content Library refreshes automatically; the new outline is also emailed to you.

### How async operations work under the hood

When you ask Eve to **write**, **brainstorm**, **repurpose**, or **generate art**, the writing engine accepts the request, validates it, and immediately returns an acknowledgement. The actual heavy work runs on a background queue — research, drafting, image generation, file delivery — and emits a **toast notification** in the bottom-right when complete.

**Sync operations** (list, retrieve, approve, list outlines, list versions, etc.) skip the queue and return inline because their data is already in the database.

If a long-running task fails (rate limit, validation error), you'll see a red toast with the failure reason, and a corresponding error row appears in **Reference → Cost Tracking** (§16).

## 21. Eve — The Voice Agent

![Eve voice widget popover](./screenshots/21-eve-widget-open.png)

Eve is the voice surface. She lives at the **bottom of the sidebar** as the **Talk to Eve** button, and she's the same agent as Chat with Eve (§20) — same tools, same memory, same writing engine. The only difference is the modality.

### What Eve is good at (and what she isn't)

**Use Eve when:**

- You're hands-free (driving, walking, cooking) and want to keep working.
- You want to brainstorm out loud — Eve handles half-finished thoughts and self-corrections gracefully.
- You want short answers spoken back ("how many chapters does *Term Limits* have?", "what genres do I have?").
- You want to **review** content live by phone (see "When Eve Calls You Back" below).
- You want to discuss revisions to an outline or chapter conversationally before committing them.

**Use chat (§20) instead when:**

- You need to paste a precise title, URL, or quote — voice transcription will mangle it.
- You're in a noisy or quiet-required space.
- The task is purely list-driven and you'd rather scan than listen.

### What happens when you click "Talk to Eve"

A popover appears with the **ElevenLabs voice widget** embedded. The widget:

1. **Requests microphone access** (first time only — the browser remembers thereafter).
2. Shows a brief **"Connecting"** state while it opens the WebRTC session.
3. Switches to a **listening orb** with a green pulse dot in the sidebar when ready.

The first call from a new device may take 3–5 seconds to connect. Subsequent calls in the same browser session are near-instant.

> **Microphone troubleshooting.** If nothing happens after the first click, check the browser address-bar mic permission. On macOS, also confirm the browser has system-level mic access (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone). On Chrome, an explicit permission prompt appears the first time and never again — you have to revoke and re-grant from `chrome://settings/content/microphone` if you accidentally denied it.

### How to speak to Eve

Just **talk normally.** Eve handles:

- Casual phrasing — *"Hey Eve, can you write me a chapter about JoJo's first floor speech?"*
- Self-correction — *"Brainstorm a — actually wait, first list my projects."* She'll do the second thing.
- Multi-step requests — *"Brainstorm a book in political comedy and then research congressional procedural rules."* She'll execute them sequentially.
- Conversational clarification — *"the one we did yesterday about the comedian"* will resolve via context if you've referenced it earlier in the same call.

Eve will:

- **Confirm before starting long tasks** — *"Got it. I'm going to brainstorm a 12-chapter outline in political comedy. This will take about a minute and you'll get an email when it's done. Anything else while we wait?"*
- **Tell you when results are ready** — both verbally and via the same toast notification chat sees.
- **Read short results out loud** — lists, confirmations, single-line answers.
- **For long content** (chapters, full drafts), tell you to check email and the Content Library — she won't try to read 5,000 words back at you.

### When Eve calls you back (the callback pattern)

Some operations trigger Eve to **call your phone**. Examples:

- *"Pull up chapter 3 of Term Limits so we can review it."*
- *"Let's talk through the outline for The Invisible Wall."*
- *"Read me JoJo's character bible."*

When you ask Eve for an interactive review, the system loads the content into her knowledge base, then dials the phone number on your account (E.164 format from Settings). Your phone rings; you answer; Eve picks up where you left off and you can revise verbally.

**While the web widget is active**, the system suppresses the phone callback automatically — instead, the content is loaded into the open widget session and refreshed in the UI. You'll see a toast: *"Eve has loaded [title]."* Close the widget to switch back to the phone-callback path.

### Closing Eve

- **Click Talk to Eve again** to close the popover.
- **Click anywhere outside the popover** — same effect.
- Closing the popover ends the voice session and the orb returns to its idle state.

The ElevenLabs widget keeps a per-session conversation; if you close and re-open within a few seconds, you may pick up where you left off, but for cleanliness it's better to treat each open as a fresh session and re-state the project context if relevant.

## 24. Troubleshooting

### "Failed to fetch dynamically imported module" / blank page after a deploy

The app auto-reloads to pick up the new version. If you still see this, hard-refresh: **Cmd + Shift + R** (Mac) or **Ctrl + Shift + R** (Windows).

### Eve doesn't connect

1. Check that you've allowed **microphone access** in your browser address bar.
2. Refresh the page and click **Talk to Eve** again.
3. If you still see "Connecting" forever, close the popover and try a different browser.

### A new chapter / outline didn't appear

Most writing tasks take **30–90 seconds**. If it's been longer than 5 minutes:

1. Check your **email** — most workflows email the result.
2. Check the **Content Library** with the right filter.
3. Re-issue the request to Eve or the chat. The system de-duplicates if it sees the same chapter is already being worked on.

### "Something went wrong" error page

Click **Reload Page**. If it persists, go to **Settings → Help → Replay Tutorial** to confirm your account is still active, or contact support.

### My genre doesn't appear in the dropdown

You may have deleted it. Check **Trash → Genres**. If it's gone, recreate it via **Reference → Genres → + New Genre**.

### A chapter is editable but won't save

The auto-save indicator at the top right shows the save state. If it stays on "Saving..." for more than 30 seconds, your network may have dropped. Press **Cmd/Ctrl + S** to force a retry.
